## 1\. Executive Summary

ADM is a large, diversified agricultural supply-chain manager, processor, and nutrition company. Its mix of commodity handling, oilseed processing, carbohydrate products, and nutrition gives it real operating breadth. \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business; Note 17 — Segment and Geographic Information]

My main question is: **If ADM’s profits are down because of lower margins, how can we tell whether that is temporary or whether the business is actually getting worse?** The evidence is mixed. The biggest concern is that total segment operating profit fell from $5.869 billion in 2023 to $3.242 billion in 2025, with Ag Services and Oilseeds down sharply in 2025 and Crushing falling from $844 million to $159 million. \[Source 1 — Note 17; MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit]

At the same time, ADM had $1.015 billion of cash and cash equivalents and reported $10.4 billion of available liquidity at year-end 2025. Nutrition improved in 2025 while several other businesses weakened. \[Source 1 — Consolidated Balance Sheets; MD\&A — Liquidity and Capital Resources; MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit]

My current call is WATCH-DEFER. I see a durable business with financial flexibility, but I do not yet see enough proof that the main margin pressure has turned around.

## 2\. What ADM Does

ADM buys crops from farmers and uses an integrated origination, logistics, and manufacturing network to turn agricultural commodities into products for food, feed, fuel, industrial, and consumer markets. \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business]

Its three reportable segments are:

* **Ag Services and Oilseeds:** crop origination, merchandising, transportation, storage, oilseed crushing, vegetable oils, and protein meals. \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business; Note 17]
* **Carbohydrate Solutions:** corn and wheat processing into sweeteners, starches, flour, ethanol, and feed ingredients. \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business; Note 17]
* **Nutrition:** human and animal nutrition ingredients and solutions. \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business; Note 17]

This is important for the investment question because ADM is not only a commodity processor. Its businesses have different end markets and margin drivers, so stronger Nutrition results can partly offset weakness elsewhere, although not necessarily enough to offset a large Crushing downturn. \[Source 1 — Note 17; MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit]

## 3\. What Looks Good

ADM has meaningful infrastructure and diversification. Its global network connects origination, transportation, processing, and end markets, which should be useful across agricultural cycles. This is my inference from the company’s described integrated network and segment mix, not a guarantee of future returns. \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business]

Liquidity is also a strength. At December 31, 2025, ADM reported $1.015 billion in cash and cash equivalents, $12.3 billion in credit lines with $9.4 billion unused, and $10.4 billion of available liquidity. \[Source 1 — Consolidated Balance Sheets; MD\&A — Liquidity and Capital Resources; Note 10]

Nutrition was a relative bright spot: 2025 segment operating profit rose to $417 million from $386 million in 2024, an 8% increase. Animal Nutrition increased $39 million, while Human Nutrition declined only $8 million. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit]

The company is also trying to lower its cost base. ADM announced targeted actions expected to produce more than $500 million of cumulative cost savings in the next three to five years, using cost optimization and portfolio simplification. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — 2025 Strategy; Note 18]

## 4\. What Concerns Me

The earnings trend is the main issue. Total segment operating profit was $3.242 billion in 2025, down $967 million, or 23%, from 2024. The multi-year direction is worse: 2025 was well below both 2024 and 2023. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit; Note 17]

Ag Services and Oilseeds was the largest problem area in 2025. Its segment operating profit declined $833 million to $1.614 billion, and Crushing declined $685 million to $159 million. ADM attributed Crushing’s weaker result to lower soy and canola crush margins and higher manufacturing costs in North America and EMEA, along with lower insurance proceeds. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — Ag Services and Oilseeds; MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit]

This matters because a drop in margins can be cyclical, but it can also show that excess industry capacity, weaker demand, costs, or trade conditions are making returns less attractive for longer. The 10-K identifies the immediate causes, but it does not prove how quickly margins will normalize. That uncertainty is the reason I am not treating a low-profit year as automatically a buying opportunity. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — Ag Services and Oilseeds]

## 6\. Key Risks and Challenges

ADM operates in an agricultural commodity business, so commodity prices, crop conditions, energy costs, transportation availability, and logistics can affect margins and operating results. \[Source 1 — Item 1A, Risk Factors]

The company also faces disruption risk from equipment failures, weather, fires, labor disputes, cyberattacks, port and waterway constraints, and other supply-chain interruptions. \[Source 1 — Item 1A, Risk Factors]

For the current thesis, the most practical risk is that Crushing margins remain weak longer than expected. Trade-policy uncertainty and lower demand for vegetable oil and biodiesel also hurt refined-products and biodiesel/refining margins in 2025. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — Ag Services and Oilseeds]

Debt deserves monitoring even though liquidity is substantial. Using my valuation definition of interest-bearing debt—$798 million short-term debt + $1.006 billion current maturities of long-term debt + $6.606 billion long-term debt—ADM had approximately $8.410 billion at December 31, 2025. This is a simple balance-sheet definition, not ADM’s stated net-debt metric. \[Source 1 — Consolidated Balance Sheets; Note 10]

## 7\. Competitive Advantages

I think ADM’s main advantages are its scale, integrated infrastructure, diversified processing network, and ability to connect farmers, global trade, manufacturing, and downstream customers. This is an analytical conclusion based on the business description rather than a claim that ADM makes using the phrase “competitive advantage.” \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business]

The company’s spread across Ag Services and Oilseeds, Carbohydrate Solutions, and Nutrition is useful because the segments serve different products and end markets. Nutrition’s 2025 improvement illustrates that diversification can help when a major commodity-linked business is weak. \[Source 1 — Item 1, Business; MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit]

However, these advantages do not remove cyclicality. A strong network can still earn less when crush margins, demand, or logistics economics are unfavorable. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — Ag Services and Oilseeds; Item 1A, Risk Factors]

## 8\. Management’s Strategy and Outlook

ADM’s 2025 strategy emphasizes execution and cost management, strategic simplification, targeted growth investment, and disciplined capital deployment. Specific priorities include improving plant efficiency, reducing third-party spending and SG\&A, reducing low-return capital spending, and using closures, divestitures, and synergies where appropriate. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — 2025 Strategy]

The cost-savings plan is potentially helpful, but I will judge it by later results. The announced target is more than $500 million of cumulative savings over the next three to five years, not an assurance that savings will fully offset weak margins. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — 2025 Strategy; Note 18]

I also note that ADM recognized a $179 million impairment related to previously capitalized internal-use software after refining its digital strategy toward more regional and agile projects. That does not decide the thesis by itself, but it is a reminder that implementation and capital allocation need to be watched. \[Source 1 — MD\&A — 2025 Strategy]

## 10\. Current Investment Call

**WATCH-DEFER.** ADM has meaningful scale, diversification, liquidity, infrastructure, and long-term competitive advantages. But total segment operating profit declined substantially from 2023 through 2025, and the 2025 weakness in Ag Services and Oilseeds—especially Crushing—is too large to ignore. \[Source 1 — Note 17; MD\&A — Segment Operating Profit; MD\&A — Liquidity and Capital Resources]

I would become more willing to initiate an investment if upcoming results show sustained recovery in Ag Services and Oilseeds, especially Crushing margins, while ADM demonstrates progress on its cost-savings plan without materially weakening its balance sheet or long-term operations.

## 11\. What I Still Need to Know

* Are soy and canola crush margins stabilizing across more than one reporting period?
* Is Ag Services and Oilseeds recovering through better underlying margins and volumes, rather than one-time items or mark-to-market timing?
* How much of the announced cost-savings plan has been realized, and what restructuring or operational tradeoffs were needed to achieve it?
* Can Nutrition keep growing profitably while its other businesses work through weaker conditions?
* How will trade policy, vegetable-oil and biodiesel demand, and manufacturing costs affect margins? \[Source 1 — MD\&A — Ag Services and Oilseeds; MD\&A — 2025 Strategy]
